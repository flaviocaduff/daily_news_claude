Hensoldt to have 'Spexer' radars built at ZF

Hensoldt, a German defence firm near Munich, will let ZF Friedrichshafen build some of its radars. Demand for drone-defence radars is higher than Hensoldt can meet.

  • The two companies signed the cooperation deal on Thursday at the Federal Economics Ministry.
  • ZF will build the 'Spexer' radars itself and deliver them directly to customers.
  • ZF will test the radars with its own system before delivery.
  • Hensoldt keeps the intellectual property, meaning the rights to the radar designs.
  • ZF is cutting jobs because of the car industry crisis. Contract production helps it use more of its capacity.
  • Many defence firms struggle to switch from single units to mass production as Western Europe rearms.
  • ZF board member Andreas Moser says ZF's skills are in demand outside the car industry.
  • Hensoldt CEO Oliver Dörre says ZF has strong high-frequency technology and mass-production experience.

Why it matters: Car suppliers are moving into defence production as demand for drone defence grows.

Lawsuits loom against UBS over inherited Credit Suisse liabilities

UBS may face direct lawsuits over Credit Suisse's collapse and 2023 rescue. A Swiss legal analysis says investors could claim damages.

  • UBS took over Credit Suisse's assets and its possible damage-claim liabilities in 2023, by law.
  • UBS paid 3 billion Swiss francs in March 2023. The article calls the bank 'Escher-Bank' but refers to Credit Suisse.
  • A parliamentary inquiry (PUK) report says UBS knew the planned state rescue before the final deal.
  • UBS chairman Colm Kelleher told the inquiry he negotiated the price with authorities, not Credit Suisse.
  • As the price rose to 3 billion, the state's loss guarantee rose from 5 to 9 billion francs.
  • UBS asked to block shareholders' right to court review of the exchange ratio. Authorities refused.
  • In late 2022 and early 2023, Credit Suisse leaders said the situation was stable. Outflows of client money continued, and the regulator FINMA knew this.
  • The Zurich Commercial Court is having Credit Suisse's value as an ongoing company assessed. Lawsuits already run in New York; the defendants deny the claims.

Why it matters: Investors could still sue UBS over the 2023 rescue, so the matter is not settled.

Swiss Post struggles with 'Klara' system: misdirected reminders, lost scans

A businessman says Swiss Post's scanning service sent him other people's mail for months. Authorities have not fixed the problem.

  • Swiss Post's ePost scanning service opens paper letters, scans them and emails them as PDFs.
  • Users pay 80 rappen (cents) per scan. The technology comes from Lucerne firm Klara, which Post bought in 2020.
  • The businessman says Post sent him PDFs meant for other recipients. Only his billing worked properly.
  • Post blames a misread 'c/o' line, the care-of address line. The postal regulator PostCom says it is not responsible.
  • The communications office Bakom did not respond. The data protection office EDÖB only automatically confirmed a complaint filed three months ago.
  • Post admits other scans went to the wrong recipient, but calls these cases 'well under one in a thousand'.
  • Over 54,000 accounts are registered on Klara. Post is reviewing how to handle c/o addresses to reduce errors.

Why it matters: Users of digital mail services could receive other people's letters if such errors continue.
