  • Chinese researchers, led by the CAS Aerospace Information Research Institute, tested wireless tech in the Q/V band at a Yunnan satellite ground station.

TLDR InfoSec

Java4Shell: Critical OpenJDK Base64 Decoder RCE Flaw (CVSS 9.2)

  • Java4Shell (CVE-2026-114938): memory-corruption bug in OpenJDK's Base64 decoder, CVSS 4.0 score 9.2 (critical).
  • Unauthenticated attackers can overwrite heap memory, leak data Heartbleed-style, or reach remote code execution.
  • Trigger: 64 'A' characters followed by '=' (128 on OpenJDK 17). The bug is in AVX-512 VBMI assembly used for fast decoding.
  • Researchers demonstrated default-config RCE in Keycloak, Jira, Confluence, Jenkins, Artifactory, Kafka and Elasticsearch; Struts and Spring Boot samples too.
  • A separate client-side exploit, shared by samczsun, compromises Java programs that open TLS connections to malicious servers.
  • AI-assisted: about one hour to find the bug, two more for a first exploit, under $200 in tokens. The excerpt mentions no patch.

TLDR DevOps

Uber's framework governs autonomous AI agents across their lifecycle

  • Uber published a framework to govern autonomous AI agents across their lifecycle, since identity and API controls cannot judge whether an agent's action fits its context.
  • Pre-deployment: agents are registered in the Uber AI Registry and scanned by Argus for security weaknesses, including MCP servers and tools.
  • Runtime: AI Guards and Agent Guard hooks check proposed actions against policies before they proceed; post-deployment adds tracing, auditing and replay.
  • Targets prompt injection, tool and memory poisoning, goal hijacking, behavioral drift and hallucination-driven actions.
  • Uber's most effective lesson: explicit per-agent intents and constraints over generic thresholds, at a cost that scales with the number of workflows.
  • Commenters question how this applies to vendor or SaaS agents where companies cannot insert their own enforcement hooks; Uber says the framework is still evolving.
