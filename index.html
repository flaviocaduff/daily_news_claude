TLDR InfoSec

Java4Shell: Critical OpenJDK Base64 Decoder Flaw (CVSS 9.2)

Security firm Calif has disclosed a critical flaw in Java that lets remote attackers run their own code. The flaw sits in OpenJDK, the free, open version of Java.

  • The flaw is in the Base64 decoder. Base64 is a common way to store binary data as text.
  • Calif gave it the ID CVE-2026-114938 and scored it 9.2 out of 10 on the CVSS 4.0 severity scale.
  • No login is needed for some attacks. Attackers can overwrite memory, read it like the 2014 Heartbleed bug, or run code.
  • The trigger is 64 letter A's followed by an equals sign. OpenJDK 17 needs 128 A's followed by the equals sign.
  • Calif says it ran code remotely in default setups of Keycloak, Atlassian Jira, Confluence, JFrog Artifactory, Jenkins and PingFederate. These need no login.
  • After login, it says it ran code in Apache Kafka, Solr, Elasticsearch, Gerrit and Spinnaker. The lists are not complete.
  • Separately, samczsun shared a client-side exploit. A Java program that connects to a malicious server can be compromised.
  • Calif says AI helped: one hour to find the bug, two more hours for a first exploit, and under $200 in AI usage fees.

Why it matters: Java runs in banking, aviation, telecoms and government systems, so unpatched servers face risk.

German coalition tax deal: what taxpayers gain in 2027

Germany's ruling parties agreed overnight to cut the electricity tax from 2027. Their income tax relief is smaller than many expected.

  • The coalition committee, the top leaders of the CDU/CSU and SPD governing parties, talked for over eight hours. They reached a deal at about 2 a.m.
  • The electricity tax (Stromsteuer) falls in 2027. It is 2.05 cents per kilowatt hour now, and the EU minimum is 0.1 cent.
  • Chancellor Friedrich Merz pushed for the electricity cut. Bild reported he nearly ended the talks before midnight; the CDU parliamentary leader described a good atmosphere.
  • The employer-friendly German Economic Institute (IW) calculated the relief. A family with two children and incomes of 100,000 and 40,000 euros gets up to 552 euros more.
  • That 552 euros is 268 from the tax reform, 192 from child benefit and allowances, and 92 from the electricity cut.
  • A single person earning 50,000 euros gross gets 155 euros more. At 100,000 euros, the gain is 253 euros.
  • Verivox, a price comparison site, estimates a family of four saves about 7.75 euros a month from the electricity cut. A single person saves about 3 euros.
  • Cold progression (kalte Progression) means inflation pushes people into higher tax brackets without real pay gains. In 2027 it costs 10.76 billion euros, but only 3.2 billion is offset.

Why it matters: Most households get only small relief in 2027, and many pay more overall.

  • MAI Code 2, for everyday coding, reached 98% of OpenAI's GPT-6.1 Sol performance at 40% of the price. It reached 96% of Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 at 5% of the price.
  • MAI Code 2 is not tuned for hard science research or very long AI tasks, Microsoft says. MAI Code 2 Flash, for routine tasks, matches cheaper rival models at 65% and 20% of their prices.
  • MAI Cyber 2 Flash is a security model. Microsoft says it is priced 86% lower than MAI Cyber 1.1 Flash on output tokens (units of AI text output).
  • MAI Cyber accounts for almost 40% of scans in MDASH, a scanning service the source does not describe.
  • MAI Image 2.7 ranks first for image editing on Artificial Analysis, an independent AI benchmark site. It beats GPT Image 2.6 Sunburst at one-fifth the price. Its Flash version costs about $10 per 1,000 images.
  • MAI Realtime 1 is Microsoft's first live speech-to-speech model. It beat GPT-Realtime 2.1 in Microsoft's tests. It starts at $0.01 per audio minute.
  • Dragon Copilot, Microsoft's medical tool, now transcribes 100% of doctor-patient conversations. It covers 10 million encounters a month and is used by a third of US physicians.
  • Decision 1.1 sends each request to the right model, so costly models run only when needed. Microsoft says it is 50 times faster and 79 times cheaper than GPT-6.1 Sol. The source names 'Jev' as a rival, which may be a typo.

Why it matters: Cheaper models could lower the costs of companies building AI tools and agents.

Microsoft Outage Traced to Latent Human-Written Windows Bug

A Microsoft team lead says a hidden bug caused a major outage, and the faulty code was not AI-written. Microsoft's full report is not out yet.

  • The account comes from a post by a developer who spoke with a Microsoft team lead. It is not an official Microsoft statement.
  • The bug was a 'latent bug' (a hidden error that stays inactive for a while). It came from a human-written code change saved about four months earlier to a Windows build.
  • The bug only switched on much later, and the source says it was very hard to spot.
  • Servers became overloaded and unreachable. The source says several other things also went wrong during the outage.
  • A developer found the root cause after spotting a memory dump that looked like an earlier error. AI diagnostic tools helped the response.
  • Twelve hours in, Microsoft still had not found why servers crashed when brought back online. The poster reads Microsoft's updates as pointing to a 'thundering herd' (many machines reconnecting at once) plus another unclear issue.
  • Microsoft plans a detailed public postmortem (a formal incident report) later. It first wants all fixes patched globally.

Why it matters: It shows how one hidden bug in old code can disrupt large computer systems.

Infoblox Confirms Breach via Compromised Third-Party Vendor

Infoblox, a cybersecurity and networking company, confirmed that hackers accessed some of its company information. They got in through a outside vendor.

  • Infoblox is privately held, so its shares are not traded on a stock market. It provides domain name lookup and user access management.
  • The company said it is investigating unauthorized access to certain company information. The access came through a compromised third-party data migration vendor.
  • Data migration means moving data from one system to another. Two people told TechCrunch the vendor provides SharePoint migration services.
  • SharePoint is Microsoft's software for sharing and storing files. Infoblox hosts files, email and infrastructure with Microsoft.
  • One source said sensitive customer data was taken. Infoblox's statement did not mention customer data.
  • Infoblox has not named the vendor. One source said Infoblox has ended its work with the vendor.
  • Infoblox says no sign that its products, cloud services, software updates or build-and-release processes were involved.
  • Infoblox activated its incident response procedures (its plan for handling security breaches). It hired outside cybersecurity experts to investigate.

Why it matters: Customers may be affected, but the company has not said how many or what data was taken.

Uber Built a Lifecycle Framework to Govern Autonomous AI Agents

Uber Engineering published how it secures AI agents. These are AI programs that take real actions, such as using tools or changing data. Uber checks whether each action fits the user's request and the agent's allowed role.

  • Normal login and access controls only check who may use a resource. They cannot judge whether an AI agent's proposed action makes sense in context.
  • Uber lists risks including prompt injection (hidden instructions that trick an AI), tool and memory poisoning, and hallucinations (AI making up false information).
  • Before deployment, each agent must be registered in Uber's AI Registry. Registration records its owner, purpose, permitted actions and risk level.
  • A tool called Argus automatically scans agent code, prompts, settings and tool connections for security weaknesses. The same check applies to MCP servers (connectors that link AI to tools).
  • While running, AI Guards check each proposed action against the agent's policies. Agent Guard hooks stop an action until the guard approves it.
  • After deployment, Uber logs every action and security decision. It can replay recorded interactions when policies change, to test the new rules.
  • Uber reports that agent-specific rules worked better than generic limits. Its main challenges were avoiding false alarms, keeping delays low and reducing setup friction for developers.
  • UN IT officer Tristan G. and AI architect Pratik Dhanave commented on LinkedIn. They said per-agent rules add the most value but cost more effort as workflows grow.

Why it matters: It shows one large company's method for controlling AI agents that act on their own.
